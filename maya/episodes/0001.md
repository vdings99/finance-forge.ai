## Topic

Registered accounts in Canada — how Canadian residents can use them to save for retirement or other purposes, and the tax treatment of each.

## Summary

- **TFSA:** No immediate tax deduction on contributions. Growth is tax-free, and withdrawals are tax-free. Contributions come from after-tax income.
- **RRSP:** Contributions are tax-deductible, giving an immediate tax reduction. Withdrawals are taxed as income. Best when you're in a higher tax bracket now and expect to be in a lower one in retirement. Name refers to retirement savings.
- **FHSA (First Home Savings Account):** Combines features of both — tax deduction on contributions, tax-free growth, and tax-free withdrawals, but only for a qualifying first home purchase. Limits: $8,000/year, $40,000 lifetime, over a 15-year participation period.
- **FHSA edge case (researched live):** If you never buy a home, the balance can be transferred directly to an RRSP or RRIF tax-free without using RRSP contribution room. The account must be closed by December 31 of the earlier of 15 years after opening or the year you turn 71. If left past the deadline, the fair market value is included in income that year — no grace period. Deductions already claimed are not clawed back.
