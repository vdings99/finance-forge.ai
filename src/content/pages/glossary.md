A reference guide to key terms used on finance-forge.ai and in Canadian tax law.

## A

**Adjusted Cost Base (ACB)**
The original purchase price of an investment plus allowable additions (such as commissions), used to calculate capital gains or losses on disposition. For identical shares bought at different times, ACB is averaged across all holdings.

## B

**Basic Personal Amount (BPA)**
A non-refundable federal and provincial tax credit available to all taxpayers. The credit reduces the tax you owe; in 2025 the federal BPA is $16,129.

## C

**Capital Gain**
The profit realized when a property or investment is sold for more than its adjusted cost base. In Canada, only a portion (the inclusion rate) of capital gains is added to taxable income.

**Capital Loss**
The loss realized when a property is sold for less than its adjusted cost base. Capital losses can only be applied against capital gains.

**CPP (Canada Pension Plan)**
A mandatory contributory pension plan for most employed Canadians. Contributions are shared equally between employee and employer; self-employed individuals pay both portions.

## D

**Dividend**
A payment made by a corporation to its shareholders out of after-tax profits. Canadian-source dividends receive preferential tax treatment through the dividend gross-up and dividend tax credit mechanism.

**Dividend Tax Credit**
A non-refundable tax credit that partially offsets the double taxation of corporate income when dividends are paid to individual shareholders.

## E

**EI (Employment Insurance)**
A federal program providing temporary income replacement to eligible workers who lose their jobs. Premiums are paid by employees and employers based on insurable earnings.

**Eligible Dividend**
A dividend paid by a Canadian public corporation (or qualifying private corporation), subject to enhanced gross-up and dividend tax credit rates compared to non-eligible dividends.

## F

**FHSA (First Home Savings Account)**
A registered account introduced in 2023 allowing first-time home buyers to save up to $8,000 per year (lifetime limit $40,000) on a tax-deductible basis, with tax-free withdrawals for a qualifying home purchase.

## M

**Marginal Tax Rate**
The rate of tax applied to the next dollar of income earned. Canada uses a progressive tax system, so the marginal rate increases as income rises through the brackets.

## N

**Net Income**
Total income minus allowable deductions such as RRSP contributions, child-care expenses, and union dues. Net income is used to calculate income-tested benefits such as OAS and the Canada Child Benefit.

**Non-Refundable Tax Credit**
A credit that reduces the tax you owe but cannot reduce your tax below zero (i.e., it will not generate a refund). Examples include the basic personal amount and the age amount.

## O

**OAS (Old Age Security)**
A monthly federal benefit available to most Canadians aged 65 and over, regardless of work history. OAS is subject to a clawback (recovery tax) if net income exceeds a threshold ($90,997 in 2025).

## R

**RDSP (Registered Disability Savings Plan)**
A long-term savings plan for Canadians with disabilities, eligible for the Canada Disability Savings Grant and Canada Disability Savings Bond from the federal government.

**RESP (Registered Education Savings Plan)**
A tax-sheltered account for saving toward a child's post-secondary education. Contributions are not deductible but growth is tax-deferred; the government provides the Canada Education Savings Grant (CESG) up to 20% on the first $2,500 per year.

**RRIF (Registered Retirement Income Fund)**
An account into which RRSP funds must be converted by age 71. The holder must withdraw a minimum amount each year, which is included in taxable income.

**RRSP (Registered Retirement Savings Plan)**
A tax-deferred savings account; contributions are deductible from income in the year made, investments grow tax-deferred, and withdrawals are taxed as income. The annual contribution limit is 18% of prior-year earned income up to the annual maximum.

## T

**TFSA (Tax-Free Savings Account)**
A registered account in which investment income and withdrawals are completely tax-free. Contributions are not deductible; unused room accumulates and is restored when withdrawals are made.

**Taxable Income**
Net income minus additional deductions (such as capital gains exemptions and loss carryforwards). Federal and provincial tax is calculated on taxable income.
