## How to read the table

Each row represents one marginal bracket — the rate that applies to each additional dollar
earned **within** that income range. Crossing a bracket boundary never raises the tax on
income already taxed in lower brackets.

The four columns show the effective marginal rate on each type of income:

- **Other income** — employment, self-employment, pension, RRSP withdrawals, interest, rent, etc.
- **Capital gains** — always one-half of the "Other income" rate, because only 50% of gains are included in taxable income in 2025.
- **Eligible dividends** — paid by public corporations and CCPCs taxed at the general corporate rate. The gross-up + dividend tax credit can produce *negative* marginal rates in lower brackets, meaning the credit exceeds the tax.
- **Non-eligible dividends** — paid by CCPCs on income taxed at the small business rate. The gross-up is smaller (15%) and the credit is less generous.

## Province notes

**Ontario** — Rates include the Ontario surtax, which adds 20% to provincial tax above $5,554 and a further 36% above $7,108. This creates additional bracket thresholds that do not appear on the provincial bracket schedule. The Ontario Health Premium (up to $900/year) is not reflected.

**Quebec** — Quebec administers its own personal income tax. The federal rates shown are reduced by a 16.5% federal abatement, which Quebec retains to fund its separate system. Rates are the effective combined amount.

**Manitoba** — The rate in the $253,414–$400,000 band (51.25%) is higher than the rate above $400,000 (50.40%) because a provincial tax reduction phases out, creating a temporary spike at the upper range.

**Newfoundland & Labrador** — NL has five high-income brackets extending past $1,128,858, resulting in Canada's highest absolute top marginal rate for "other income" at 54.80%.

## Federal rate change in 2025

The lowest federal bracket rate was reduced from **15% to 14%** effective July 1, 2025. The table uses the blended annual rate of **14.5%** for the first bracket, which is what you would apply to a full calendar-year income calculation. For a part-year resident or a split-year scenario, apply 15% to income earned before July 1 and 14% after.
