## Federal corporate rates

Canada's federal corporate tax is structured around three primary rates:

| Rate Type | Rate | Income Eligible |
|---|---:|---|
| General (active business) | **15.0%** | Active business income in excess of the SBD limit |
| Small business (CCPC) | **9.0%** | First $500,000 of active business income of a CCPC |
| CCPC investment income | **38⅔%** | Passive/investment income earned by a CCPC |

**Key mechanisms:**
- The **general rate** results from the 38% basic rate, less a 10% provincial abatement, less a 13% general rate reduction.
- The **small business deduction (SBD)** reduces the federal rate to 9% on the first $500,000 of *active* business income for Canadian-controlled private corporations (CCPCs). The SBD phases out for CCPCs with more than $10M in passive income, and is reduced for associated corporations sharing the $500K limit.
- **CCPC investment income** is taxed at 38⅔% (28% regular + 10⅔% refundable). The 10⅔% is refundable when the corporation pays taxable dividends — this creates the "refundable dividend tax on hand" (RDTOH) mechanism.
- **Manufacturing & Processing (M&P)** income qualifies for the M&P credit, which effectively makes the federal M&P rate equal to the general rate (15%).

## Integrated personal + corporate rates

The integrated rate shows the total tax paid on $1.00 of corporate pre-tax income when
the after-tax remainder is distributed to the shareholder as a dividend and taxed at the
**top personal marginal rate** in that province.

**Formula:**

```
Integrated rate = Corp rate + (1 − Corp rate) × Personal top dividend rate
```

Integration is designed to make shareholders roughly indifferent between earning income
directly (salary) vs. through a corporation (dividends). Perfect integration would produce
the same after-tax dollar regardless of the vehicle. In practice, results vary by province.

<Callout type="tip">
A positive "Difference vs. Salary" means paying salary is slightly more tax-efficient at the top bracket. A negative means the corporate + dividend route is marginally cheaper. In most provinces, the difference is small (under 2%) at the top rate. The real advantage of incorporation is often **deferral** — retaining corporate income at the lower corporate rate for years before distributing it — rather than a permanent rate reduction.
</Callout>

## How the small business deduction phases out

The SBD is reduced when a CCPC (or associated group) has **passive investment income** exceeding $50,000 in the prior year. The SBD is fully eliminated at $150,000 of passive income. This "passive income trap" is a key planning consideration for incorporated business owners who also hold investment portfolios inside their corporation.

Additionally, where associated corporations share the $500,000 SBD limit (e.g., two related companies), the combined active business income eligible for the small business rate is capped at $500,000 across all associates.
